# Cat Skin Diseases Knowledge Base

## Scabies

### Description

Feline scabies (Notoedric Mange) is a contagious skin disease caused by the microscopic mite _Notoedres cati_. These mites burrow beneath the epidermis layer of the skin, triggering an inflammatory reaction and intense itching.

### Symptoms

- Extreme itching (the cat constantly scratches or bites itself)
- Thick, hard, grayish-yellow crusts on the ear margins, face, and neck
- Severe hair loss on the head and neck areas
- Skin appears thickened, wrinkled, and prone to abrasions

### Causes

Infestation of the parasitic mite _Notoedres cati_ (or rarely _Sarcoptes scabiei_), which spreads through direct contact between animals.

### Treatment

- Isolate the cat from other pets
- Administer antiparasitic/spot-on treatments (such as selamectin or fluralaner) as recommended by a veterinarian
- Bathe with anti-mite/antiseptic shampoo according to medical prescription

### Prevention

- Maintain the cleanliness of the cage and surrounding environment
- Quarantine new cats before ensuring they are free from parasites
- Regularly wash bedding and cat accessories with hot water

## Ringworm

### Description

Ringworm (dermatophytosis) is a fungal infection affecting the keratinized layers of the skin, hair, and nails in cats. This disease is **zoonotic** (can be transmitted to humans and other animals).

### Symptoms

- Circular patches of hair loss (alopecia) with reddish edges
- Scaly, dry skin, severe dandruff, or thin crust formation
- Brittle and easily broken fur around the head, ears, and forelegs
- Mild to moderate itching

### Causes

Infection by dermatophyte fungi, most commonly _Microsporum canis_ or _Trichophyton mentagrophytes_, transmitted through direct contact or fungal spores on contaminated objects (_fomites_).

### Treatment

- Isolate the cat in a dedicated, easy-to-clean room
- Topical antifungal therapy (ointments/creams) or medicated shampoos (_ketoconazole/chlorhexidine_)
- Oral antifungal therapy for extensive infections under veterinary supervision

### Prevention

- Regularly disinfect cages, grooming brushes, and play areas
- Minimize room humidity in the housing area
- Avoid direct contact without gloves when handling infected cats

## Flea Allergy

### Description

Flea Allergy Dermatitis (FAD) is a hypersensitive inflammatory skin condition triggered by proteins in the saliva of fleas (_Ctenocephalides felis_) when biting the cat's skin.

### Symptoms

- Severe itching around the lower back, base of the tail, neck, and inner thighs
- Presence of _flea dirt_ (flea feces resembling black pepper/sand granules at the base of the fur)
- Small, crusted red bumps (_miliary dermatitis_)
- Hair loss due to repeated biting or licking by the cat

### Causes

An allergic immune response to flea bites and flea saliva. A single bite can trigger severe inflammation in sensitive cats.

### Treatment

- Apply topical spot-on flea treatments specifically formulated for cats (e.g., fipronil, imidacloprid, selamectin)
- Comb the coat regularly using a fine-toothed flea comb
- Administer antihistamines or anti-inflammatory medications prescribed by a veterinarian to relieve severe itching

### Prevention

- Administer preventive flea treatments regularly every 1–3 months
- Vacuum mattresses, sofas, and carpets frequently
- Wash cat bedding regularly in hot water

## Health

### Description

The cat's skin and coat condition appear normal and show no signs of fungal infection, scabies mite infestation, or flea allergy.

### Symptoms

- Clean, dense, and vibrant coat
- Skin is free from red patches, thick crusts, or flea dirt
- The cat does not display excessive scratching or biting behavior

### Causes

Normal physiological condition and a healthy skin barrier system.

### Treatment

Does not require specific medical intervention.

### Prevention

- Provide high-quality nutrition that supports skin and coat health (rich in Omega-3 & Omega-6)
- Maintain routine grooming through regular brushing
- Conduct routine veterinary check-ups and scheduled vaccinations
